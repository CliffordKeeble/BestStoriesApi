# The laptop rerun of 11 October 2026

On 10 October every fixed-rate row from the laptop was invalid: the load generator of that day slept between sends, and on Windows a 1 ms sleep takes about 15.6 ms. This is the rerun with the fixed generator, which measures its sleep at start and spins on a coarse timer. It was run from clean clones by the same second operator. The three tables are the tool's console output as that operator reported it, verbatim; the raw files stayed on that machine.

## The machine and the conditions

- AMD Ryzen 5 PRO 7535U (6 cores, 12 threads), Windows 11 (10.0.26200), .NET SDK 10.0.401, runtime 10.0.12, Release builds.
- **On mains** throughout (`PowerLineStatus` Online at the start and end of each run), Balanced power plan, no core pinning.
- The client, the server and the fake Hacker News all on that machine, over loopback. Visual Studio, Explorer and OneDrive were running. Sampled during the second "after" run only, they took up to about half a core between them.
- **Builds:** after = `0f21cc8`, before = `4ee5907` (the first version). The after build passed 46 of 46 tests with 0 warnings.
- **Order:** after, before, after again. Each suite: open loop at 2,000, 5,000 and 7,500/s and a closed loop of 64 connections, for `n=10` and `n=200`; 5 s warm-up, then 20 s measured. A fresh fake for each run.

## What the generator says about itself

Every run's machine line says `Thread.Sleep(1)` took 16.0 to 16.1 ms, so the dispatcher spun. **Of the 18 open-loop rows, 10 kept their schedule** (send lag p99 0.04 to 0.69 ms) **and 8 did not** (1.35 to 8.71 ms). The tool marks those ⚠, and they are not quoted anywhere.

The failures are not tied to a rate. The first after run failed `n=200` at 2,000/s and passed it at 7,500/s; the second did the opposite. Why is not known: interruptions on a shared machine are the likely reading, and it was not tested. On the before build the slower server also competes with the spinning client for cores: four of its six rows failed, two of them by a wide margin (send lag p99 8.30 and 8.71 ms).

## Read with the ⚠ rows left out

- **Closed loop, the only complete before-and-after comparison:** `n=10` 56,391/s before, 84,406 and 89,606 after (1.50 and 1.59 times). `n=200` 22,468/s before, 51,601 and 38,533 after (2.30 and 1.72 times). The two after runs are 25% apart at `n=200`, so the gain there is a range, not one figure.
- **Open loop, the one rate valid in all three runs,** `n=10` at 2,000/s: p99 1.377 ms before, 0.459 and 0.411 ms after.
- **After, every valid row:** medians 0.103 to 0.226 ms.
- **No before latency can be quoted for `n=200`:** all three of its open-loop rows are ⚠.
- **Hacker News:** 603 calls in 3 refreshes, 201 per refresh, in every run.
